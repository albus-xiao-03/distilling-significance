## 1. Goal and hypothesis

**Goal.** Take an open **customer-behaviour** dataset (Amazon Reviews 2023, Video_Games) and do the following:

1. reorganise it as **behavioural sequences**: each customer's observations (products they engaged with) and their decisions about them (buying, then rating), in BehaviorGPT's input format;
2. let BehaviorGPT, **unchanged (no fine-tuning, no retraining)**, **decide how significant each observation is** for that person, from what they did before;
3. keep the original dataset and add **BehaviorGPT's significance** as the main outcome, giving a significance-labelled dataset for supervised learning;
4. characterise those labels, check that BehaviorGPT predicts this domain's behaviour well, and test whether a small supervised student can learn the labels ("distilling significance").

**What "significance" means here.** We do **not** define it. BehaviorGPT's job is predicting what a person acts on next, so the significance of an observation is **how strongly BehaviorGPT, given this person's history, expects them to act on it, beyond what it would expect with no history**. Both the score and the yes/no decision come from the model's own outputs, with no thresholds tuned on human labels (§6).

**Hypothesis.** A pretrained behaviour model, given a suitable representation of a person's sequence, yields a consistent and informative significance signal with no task-specific training. How the sequence is represented (views vs. orders, sessions, whether disliked purchases count) changes that signal; this is the representation experiment.

**What would count as a result.** Either outcome is reportable:
- *Positive:* the model predicts held-out behaviour well above baselines, its significance labels are stable and informative, and a cheap student reproduces them.
- *Negative:* any of these fails. We report where and by how much.

## 4. Core idea: let BehaviorGPT decide significance

BehaviorGPT answers one question: *"given what this person did, how likely are they to act on item X next?"* We use exactly that and nothing more:

1. **The human decisions are already in the data.** A customer bought (and reviewed) a product: that is a choice. Their rating is their own verdict on it. The sequence is written so BehaviorGPT reads these decisions (§5.2).
2. **The significance of an observation is BehaviorGPT's expectation of the person acting on it, given their history, relative to no history.** This separates "important to *this* person, given what they did" from "popular with everyone" (§6).
3. **The model also makes the decision.** An observation is significant when the person's history raises it in BehaviorGPT's ranking (lift > 0). There is no threshold to tune and no human label involved (§6.3, confirmed as D14).
4. **Never score an observation from a history that already contains it**, because seen items are pushed down on purpose. 5-core has no repeated user–item pairs, so each observation is scored on the strictly earlier history.

### 5.2 Sequences (Significance Sequence Format, SSF)

One sequence per customer, ordered by time:
- **observations** = the products they reviewed (a purchase proxy);
- **decisions** = buying them, and the rating they gave.

Purchases on the same day form one **session**. Every observation is scored from the history **strictly before its session**, capped at the last *K* events (K = 50, adjusted in Phase 0).

| Variant | How the history is written | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **R0** native | Every earlier purchase as `View`, one session | The plainest mapping |
| **R1** choice | Every earlier purchase as `View` + `Order` (buying is a choice) | Does encoding the decision as an order sharpen the signal? |
| **R2** sessions | R1, split into one session per day (**SDK-only** multi-session) | Does session structure matter? |
| **R3** verdict-aware | R2, but purchases the customer rated ≤ 2 are **left out** of later histories ("taking a step back", llms.txt principle 6) | Does respecting the customer's own verdict change significance? *(This uses the rating only as recorded history, never as a target.)* |
| *R5 (optional)* | R2, with low-rated purchases as `AddToCart` → `RemoveFromCart` instead of left out (**SDK-only** events) | Alternative encoding of a rejected purchase |

The SSF records (JSONL, git-ignored) hold `customer_id`, the session time, the history per variant, and the observations to score. Original columns travel alongside but are **never sent** to BehaviorGPT except as history events.

## 6. Significance computation (model-native)

### 6.1 One call per customer-session

For a session with observations `O` (≤ 10 per call; larger sessions are split) and history `H`:

```
complete(H, limit=len(O)+len(R), filters={"id": O ∪ R}, catalog_id=<Video_Games catalog>)
```

`R` is a **fixed uniform reference sample** of ~90 catalog items, drawn once from the full item list, which we know because we built the catalog. Reference items that appear in `H` are dropped for that call.

One call returns the observations' scores and the reference distribution under the same history.

- **Prior table:** `s(o│∅)` for every catalog item and every reference item, at about catalog size / 100 calls (≤ 1,000).
- **Every call is:**
  - cached in SQLite under `.temp/cache/` (resumable);
  - retried with backoff on 429/5xx;
  - throttled to the limit measured in Phase 0;
  - sent without `user_id`, so nothing is recorded on the account.

### 6.2 Per-observation outputs

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `score_hist` | `s(o│H)`, BehaviorGPT's raw score given the history |
| `z_hist` | Robust z-score of `s(o│H)` against the reference sample under the same `H` (median/MAD, as in `behaviorgpt-anomaly`): how much `o` stands out for this person |
| `z_prior` | The same against the reference sample with no history: how much `o` stands out for anyone |
| `pct_hist`, `pct_prior` | Share of reference items scoring below `o`, with and without the history |
| **`significance`** | **`lift = z_hist − z_prior`**: how much this person's history raises `o`, as judged by BehaviorGPT. **The main outcome.** |
| **`significant`** | **`lift > 0`**: the model's own decision, parameter-free (D14) |
| `confidence` | `|lift|` scaled by the reference spread |
| `cold_start` | True for a customer's first session (empty history): only prior fields, `significant` = null |
| `variant`, `history_len`, `session_size` | Provenance |

### 6.3 Why this decision rule

- It uses only BehaviorGPT outputs.
- It needs no threshold: zero means "the history does not change how much the model expects this item".
- It compares like with like: the same reference items, the same model, with and without the person.

Alternatives (D14) are a model-top-share rule (`pct_hist ≥ q`) or **continuous only** (no binary label).

### 6.4 Choosing the representation

R0–R3 (and R5 if wanted) run on a **stratified sample** (≈ 5 % of customers, every split). Then the chosen variant runs on **all** rows (D16). The choice criterion is behavioural, not a significance definition: the variant under which BehaviorGPT best predicts customers' **actual next purchases** on the train/valid windows (§8 E1).

## 8. Evaluation

We do not define significance, so nothing is scored against a human "significance" label. Instead:

| # | Question | How | Baselines |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Does BehaviorGPT do its job in this domain? (teacher validity) | For each **test**-split purchase, the rank of the item the customer actually bought next, from one extra top-100 head call per test session plus the reference sample: hit@10/@100, MRR, mean percentile | Random; popularity (train-window counts); item-to-item co-occurrence from the train window |
| **E2** | Does the representation matter? | E1 per variant (on the sample); agreement of `significant` between variants (Cohen's κ); rank correlation of `significance` | — |
| **E3** | What do the labels look like? | Base rate; distributions; confidence; by split, category, price band, history length | — |
| **E4** | How does BehaviorGPT's significance relate to the customers' own signals? (descriptive only) | Spearman / AUC of `significance` against `rating` (and `helpful_vote` / `verified_purchase` if D15); reported, **never used to tune anything** | — |
| **E5** | Distillation: can a cheap model learn BehaviorGPT's significance? | A scikit-learn logistic regression (and optionally gradient boosting) on non-BehaviorGPT features: title TF-IDF, category one-hots, store, price, the customer's past category shares, history length, item train-window popularity. Train on **train** rows' labels, select on **valid**, report on **test**: AUC and κ against BehaviorGPT's `significant`, R² against `significance`. | Majority class; popularity-only feature set |

All metrics come with **95 % bootstrap CIs** over customers.

**Hackathon success targets:**
1. The full Video_Games data is labelled end to end, cached and reproducible.
2. E1: BehaviorGPT beats popularity on next-purchase rank, with the CI excluding a tie.
3. E2: the representation effect is reported in whichever direction.
4. E5: the student reaches AUC ≥ 0.7 against BehaviorGPT's labels on test (a target, not a guarantee).

| **D16** | Representation comparison | **Compare R0–R3 on a 5 % sample, then run the chosen variant on all data** · run every variant on all data (~4× calls and time) | **Sample, then full** |
| **D17** | API volume on the free key | Approve ≈ 1.0M ranking calls at up to 8–16 parallel workers, throttled to the measured limit · cap it lower · ask Unbox AI first | **Approve, throttled**; I stop and ask if Phase 0 shows limits that would stretch it beyond ~1 day |

## 11. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| ~1M calls exceed undocumented rate limits or fair use | Slow or blocked run | Phase 0 measurement; throttling; cache + resume; D17; contact Unbox AI if needed |
| Amazon images not fetchable by Unbox's servers | Embed job fails | ~300-row slice first; fall back to text-only rows (allowed) |
| 5-core item set > 100k | Doesn't fit one catalog | Both free slots + `catalog_ids` |
| Reference-sample noise (~90 items) | Noisy z-scores | Robust median/MAD; the same `R` everywhere; Phase 0 stability check; enlarge `R` with a second filtered call if needed |
| Seen-item suppression | Biased scores | Score only from strictly earlier sessions; 5-core has no repeated user–item pairs; reference items in `H` dropped |
| Cold start (first session) | No history | `cold_start` flag; prior fields only; `significant` = null |
| History timing is synthetic (SDK re-stamps) | Time features lost | Accepted; order preserved |
| Significance looks like popularity | Uninformative labels | Lift against the no-history prior removes the popularity component by construction; E3/E4 report it |
| Data leaks into the public repo | Licence / terms breach | `outputs/`, `.temp/` git-ignored; synthetic test fixtures; data card and reports aggregate-only; review the diff before every commit; no push without permission |
| Leakage across time | Inflated E1/E5 | Histories strictly earlier; McAuley global time split; popularity baselines from the train window only; catalog without popularity columns |

## 12. Potential further plan: Track A (MIND news)

Kept for a later plan (e.g. R2). Not part of R1.

- **Data:** MIND-small on HF `yjw1029/MIND` (`MINDsmall_train.zip` 53.0 MB, `MINDsmall_dev.zip` 30.9 MB). Each impression records every headline shown with click 1/0, plus the reader's click history. Full MIND has ~160k articles (more than one catalog), so small only.
- **Why it is interesting:** explicit shown-and-passed-over decisions. It would test the same model-decided significance with dismissals available: a separate "passed over" context and contrastive scores.
- **Prerequisites:**
  - the owner accepts the Microsoft Research License on HF and adds a read-only `HF_TOKEN` to `.env` (the repo is gated; currently 401);
  - a decision on uploading MIND headlines to Unbox AI (the licence forbids distribution and is silent on processing services);
  - MIND-derived data never committed.
- **Reuse:** the R1 pipeline (SSF, significance scorer, data card, student) is dataset-agnostic; Track A needs a MIND reader and catalog adapter (headlines as text-only items).
